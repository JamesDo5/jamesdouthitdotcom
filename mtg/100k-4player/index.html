b. Colour pairs

Field win rate is the record of the decks the builder chose to build in a pair, so each pair is measured only on pools that already favour it; that pulls every pair toward 50% and moves colour strength into share. Strength on a random pool builds the same sampled pools in every pair and plays each build against the same field decks, so it estimates how good the pair is for a pool chosen at random; it sits below 50% because most of those builds are not the pool’s best pair. On the same pools, a pair’s build scored +9.7 points higher where the builder chose that pair than where it did not (weighted by how often each pair was chosen; GW: 51.9% when chosen vs 41.9% when not).

Draws count half. The 95% CI is mean ± 1.96 sd/√n over pool means. A pair is buildable when the pool has at least 23 castable cards in it. Champion scoring. A deck’s rare power B is the sum over its rare, mythic and SDS nonland cards of max(0, grade − 2.5) with ratings v4; the 3,405 undefeated event decks have median B 1.91 (mean 2.07, 2.31 rares + mythics) against 1.29 (mean 1.48, 2.02) for all 500,000 decks. A logistic fit on 200,000 of the run’s own games gives P(A wins) = σ(0.180·seat + 0.104·(BA − BB)), so a B lead of 1, 2 or 4 is worth 52.6%, 55.2% or 60.2%. The pool-adjusted scoring credits each game with its actual minus its expected score under that fit (Swiss pairs and ranks by the sum, raw match points break ties, the top 8 is seeded by it and decided by match wins). Champion scoring

Champion scoring. A deck’s rare power B is the sum over its rare, mythic and SDS nonland cards of max(0, grade − 2.5) with ratings v4; the 3,405 undefeated event decks have median B 1.91 (mean 2.07, 2.31 rares + mythics) against 1.29 (mean 1.48, 2.02) for all 500,000 decks. A logistic fit on 200,000 of the run’s own games gives P(A wins) = σ(0.180·seat + 0.104·(BA − BB)), so a B lead of 1, 2 or 4 is worth 52.6%, 55.2% or 60.2%. Deck rules

  • 40 cards: 23 nonland + 17 lands, exactly one of the 10 two-colour pairs (no voluntary splash; a third colour only fills a pool with fewer than 23 castable cards). Any number of copies of an opened card.
  • Deckbuilder: per pair, a greedy pick plus single-swap hill climb on the sum of card ratings minus soft penalties (mana curve guideline 1–2 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 2–4 by MV, at least 13 creatures, colour strain) plus synergy terms (Station, Strange new worlds, landfall); the best pair wins.
  • Lands: on-colour nonbasics first (Planet duals, shocks), at most one colourless utility land, basics split by coloured pips with largest-remainder rounding and at least 5 sources per colour.

Game simplifications

  • 1v1, 20 life, full turn structure, one land per turn, summoning sickness, state-based actions after every action batch, legend rule, decking loses.
  • No stack or priority passing: instants and activated abilities are used in fixed windows (own main phases, after blockers are declared with the defender first, the opponent’s end step, and a single response window for spell-targeting effects).
  • London mulligan at most twice: 7 → 6 cards → 3 cards (the second mulligan bottoms 4).
  • Game cap: 40 player-turns (20 each) → a draw worth half a win; a 500-action-per-turn loop guard also ends the game as a draw. Each event: 5 pools → 5 decks → round robin, each pairing plays 2 games (20 games), so 8 games per deck. Who goes first is random each game.
  • Both players are the same heuristic AI (board evaluation, combat simulation for attacks and blocks, greedy sequencing); its mistakes are the same on both sides but still shape which cards look good.

Rating method and caveats

  • Deckbuilding ratings start from v0 = 0.7 × a curated 0–5 Limited grade + 0.3 × a text heuristic, then are refined from simulated GIH win rates (shrunk with k = 30 toward the mean, rank-mapped to the grade scale, blended 50/50 with the previous version).
  • Card win rates are games-in-hand (GIH) win rates: games where at least one copy was drawn, including the opening hand. They are shrunk toward the mean with k = 30 and only ranked from n ≥ 200 games and an on-colour inclusion of at least 15% (decks playing the card / pools that held it and could cast it). The floor keeps a card that wins when the AI plays it but that the builder rarely chooses from ranking first on a small, selected sample; such cards are listed separately under the tables.
  • Selection bias: a card is only measured in decks that chose to play it, and drawing a card correlates with longer games. GIH measures “how good is the card when you play it”, not how good it would be in any deck; pair win rates include the builder’s choice of which pools end up in each pair.
  • Results are for this simulator’s AI and rules subset, not for human play.

6. Task 14: splash builder

BuilderConfig.allow_splash (default off) lets each pair try each third colour as a splash. Splash candidates are cards that need the third colour only through plain pips (splash_max_pips 1, no hybrid that needs it), rated rating − splash_card_penalty, at most splash_max_cards (3) of them, picked inside the same greedy + hill climb. A colour is only tried when one of its candidates beats the deck's 23rd card and its optimistic gain can lift the pair above the best objective so far. Lands: splash sources are nonbasics making the splash colour plus another deck colour (or only deck colours), then basics up to splash_min_sources (3); Treasure / any-colour producers among the spells count half a source each, at most one source in total. Main colours keep ≥ 5 sources. splash_basic_penalty (0.0) and splash_source_penalty (0.5, for sources the 17 lands cannot supply) are left at their defaults. Per-deck card tallies in the record would remove the loss but need an event.py change.

Round 2 pre: pair strength on random pools (scripts/pair_strength.py, report section b)

A pair's field win rate is the record of the decks the builder chose to build in that pair, so it is measured only on pools that already favour the pair (the effect on the treated). That pulls every pair toward 50% (47–52% here) and moves colour strength into share (green is in about 85% of decks). scripts/pair_strength.py removes the selection: it takes N = 1000 pools sampled uniformly from a run's decks (seed 1), builds each pool in all 10 pairs with the run's builder config and ratings (sim.deck.builder.build_with_report with the pair forced; every pair also tries its splash variants when the run allowed splashing), and plays every build against the same K = 16 gauntlet decks from the run's field in both seats with common game seeds. A pair with fewer than 23 castable cards in a pool is not buildable there and is skipped. Strength is the mean pool win rate (draws half); the CI is mean ± 1.96·sd/√n over pool means. Both runs were measured from a clean worktree at d63c2f8 (AI t7, mulligan 7 → 6 → 3, ratings v4, as the runs used); the forced build of the pair the builder chose reproduced the recorded deck in 100% of pools.

Strength sits below 50% because the gauntlet is the field (each pool's best choice) while most forced builds are not the pool's best pair. "When chosen" / "not chosen" split the same pair's strength by whether the builder chose that pair for the pool; the gap is the selection effect.
